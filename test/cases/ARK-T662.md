## Objective

Prove that the three writes of the Companions page (approve a ticket, dismiss with a reason, ask) go through routes that accept only a POST from the hub's own page, touch only a listed companion's files and the proposed tickets that name it, and that ADR 0009's rule, amended for these three routes, still holds for every other route. Serves ARK-480/AC7. For open-in-orca it also proves that the answers do not depend on host load: the POST goes out only after the hub's snapshot lists `term-worker` as live. Serves ARK-504/AC1. The write-method sweep (step 6) and the run hub of step 9 wait for the hub's first Ark read, with a bound, before they expect 200 from the run route, so a first read longer than the 250 ms bound of `readyOrLoading` (hub/server.ts) is no failure and a first read that never ends fails with its last status, not a hang. Serves ARK-620/AC1.

## Preconditions

- A real hub on a temp `ARK_HOME` (the harness of `test/hub.test.ts`), the fake orca of ARK-T660 (logs `send`, `task-create`), project `p` with the companion `cortana` listed, a ticket `ARK-901` at `status: proposed` with `source: cortana`, a ticket `ARK-902` at `status: proposed` with no `source`, a ticket `ARK-903` at `status: ready`.
- For steps 7 and 8, a hub on a project with no companion and an Orca fake that lists the live run lead `term-lead` and the live worker `term-worker` of an open run (two connected terminals, as `orcaAnswersFor` builds them) and logs every `terminal` call (the harness of `test/hub.test.ts`). Step 7 posts `term-worker`. Before the first POST, `/api/snapshot` lists an agent with `terminal: "term-worker"` and `presence: "live"`; the wait is bounded, the test's own timeout is longer than that wait, and a timeout of the wait fails the test with the last snapshot (no silent exit, no bare Bun timeout). For step 9, two setups: for the first half, the companion hub of the first precondition (project `p` listing `cortana`) with Orca down; for the second half, a run hub with a run and no companion, with the Orca fake of `test/hub.test.ts` in its down mode. For step 10, the load of AC1: the full `cli` suite of `ark.yml` (`bun test test --parallel=<cores>`) running on the same host, and another `ark` test suite (for example `playwright`) running next to it.
- Authorities: the hub's `127.0.0.1:<port>` as in `openInOrca`.
- For step 6 and the second half of step 9 (ARK-620/AC1): the Ark home also holds the large project of `test/hub-home-lag.test.ts` (1,620 run folders in the real shape, written by the same builder), written once per test file and read only, so the hub's first Ark read lasts well over 250 ms on an idle host and the run route answers 503 `loading` at first. No change under `hub/`. ARK-T776 proves on the same home that the hold is real (its first answer is 503 `loading`), so a fixture the reader skips cannot make this proof pass by accident. The test's timeout is longer than the poll bound of 10 s plus the hub start.

## Steps

1. For each of the three routes, POST a good body with `Origin` and `Host` equal to the hub's authority.
2. For each route, POST the same body with no `Origin`, with a foreign `Origin`, and with a foreign `Host`.
3. POST approve for `ARK-902` (no `source`), `ARK-903` (ready), an unknown id, `../tickets/ARK-901` and `..%2f..%2fark.yml`; POST dismiss and ask for `ghost` and `../x`; POST dismiss with bad JSON and with an empty reason.
4. Limits, straight to the routes with no page: POST dismiss with a reason of 400 characters and ask with a question of 400 characters, each made of 4-byte characters (1600 bytes of text); POST dismiss and ask with 401 characters; POST dismiss with a body of 4097 bytes.
5. Make `ark.yml` invalid (`companions: 3`). GET the page's data route, `/api/snapshot` and `/`.
6. On the large home, poll GET `/api/run/<run>` until its answer is not the 503 `loading` (bound 10 s, an event wait with no fixed sleep). Then send POST, PUT, PATCH and DELETE to every served route (`/`, `/api/snapshot`, `/events`, `/api/companions`, a run route, the assets), to routes that do not exist, and to the three companion routes; send GET, PUT, PATCH and DELETE to each companion route and to `open-in-orca`. This is the write-method sweep of `test/hub.test.ts`, which this case takes over from `ARK-277-AC2`.
7. Open in Orca, from the hub's own page: POST `{"terminal":"term-worker"}` to `open-in-orca` once with the hub's `Origin` and `Host`, once with a foreign `Origin`, once with no `Origin`, once with a foreign `Host`, and once with `Origin` and `Host` the hub's own again through a raw socket. POST a terminal that Orca did not list, the text `--help; rm -rf /`, and the number 42. POST a terminal of 1025 characters.
8. Make the Orca fake fail `terminal switch`, and POST `{"terminal":"term-worker"}` with the hub's own `Origin` again.
9. With Orca down (every Orca call exits non-zero) and project `p` listing `cortana`: GET the data route of the Companions page; POST ask for `cortana` and read the Orca calls it made; POST dismiss and approve; make Orca answer again and POST the same ask. Then, on a hub with a run, the large home and Orca down, poll GET `/api/run/<run>` as in step 6, GET `/`, `/nope`, `/api/snapshot` and the run's route, POST open-in-orca for `term-worker`, and compare the run's `events.ndjson` with what it held before. The poll replaces the snapshot loop of this test, which already waits for the first Ark read (it loops while `sources.ark.ok` is false) but in fixed `Bun.sleep(100)` steps with no bound.
10. Load: while the full `cli` suite runs and another `ark` test suite runs next to it (the precondition), run steps 7 and 8 as one test twelve times in parallel (12 processes of the one test), and note how each repeat ended.

## Expected results

- Step 1: approve sets `ARK-901` to `status: ready` (one line changed) and no `task-create`; dismiss appends the reason to `companions/cortana/dismissed.md`; ask sends one message to the companion's worker. Each answers 200 with a JSON result.
- Step 2: every one of the nine requests answers 403, and no file changed and no call was logged (a missing Origin is refused here, unlike on a GET).
- Step 3: approve of `ARK-902`, `ARK-903`, the unknown id and both traversal ids answers 404 or 400 and changes no file (`ARK-902` and `ARK-903` are not a companion's proposal); a name `ghost` or `../x` answers 404 or 400 and writes no file anywhere; bad JSON answers 400; an empty reason answers 400 and writes nothing.
- Step 4: the 400-character reason and question answer 200 and are written or sent whole (these bodies are over 1024 bytes, so they also prove the raised cap); the 401-character reason and question answer 400 with a JSON error that names the 400-character limit, and nothing is written or sent; the 4097-byte body answers a clear 413 or 400 with a reason, not a dropped connection, and nothing is written.
- Step 5: with an invalid `ark.yml` the data route answers with an error body for the page's own message, and the snapshot and `/` answer 200 as before.
- Step 6: the poll ends with a status that is not 503 `loading` before its bound; at base (no poll) the first answer of the run route on the large home is 503 and the case fails, which is the red proof of ARK-620/AC1. A poll that reaches its bound fails with the route, the bound and the last status and body in its message, never with a bare Bun timeout. Then every served route answers a GET with 200; every route but `open-in-orca` and the three companion routes answers a write method with 404 or 405; each companion route and `open-in-orca` answers a GET, PUT, PATCH and DELETE with 405. The test `ARK-277-AC2` that pinned the old rule is converted to this case's id with the amended rule, not re-pinned.
- Step 7: the first request and the raw request with the hub's own authority answer 200 and Orca logs exactly one `terminal switch --terminal term-worker` for each; the foreign `Origin`, the missing `Origin` and the foreign `Host` answer 403 and Orca logs no further `terminal switch`; the unlisted terminal, the option-like text and the number 42 answer 400; the 1025-character terminal answers 413, because this route keeps its own 1024-byte limit although the server takes 4096 bytes for the companion routes; the Orca log holds two `terminal switch` lines and no `terminal send` (the route never types text into a terminal).
- Step 8: the answer is 502.
- Step 9: the data route answers `orca: unavailable` and every companion row has state `unavailable`, never `stopped`; the ask answers 502 with a non-empty error, delivers no message, and Orca saw no `send`, `task-create`, `worker-start` or `worker-stop` call from it; dismiss and approve answer 200 (they touch only files); the same ask with Orca back answers 200 and one message is delivered (so the refusal came from Orca being down). On the run hub: the poll ends as in step 6 (Orca down does not hold the Ark read), and a poll that reaches its bound fails with the route, the bound and the last status and body, where the old loop hung to the Bun timeout; `/`, `/api/snapshot` and the run route answer 200 and `/nope` answers 404; open-in-orca answers 400, because the hub knows no live terminal, and Orca logs no `terminal switch`; `events.ndjson` equals what it held before. This half has no red proof at base: the old loop also ends only after the first Ark read, so it answers 200 on the large home too. Step 6 is the only red proof of ARK-620/AC1.
- Step 10: all twelve repeats pass while both loads run, and each posts only after the snapshot listed `term-worker` live; none answers 400 where step 7 expects 200. A repeat whose terminal never goes live within the bound fails with the last snapshot in its message, not with a bare status mismatch or a Bun timeout.
- Step 6 and step 9 under load (ARK-620/AC1): the two tests (`-t "ARK-T662 every route"` and `-t "ARK-T662 with orca down the hub keeps serving"` of `test/hub.test.ts`; each pattern matches one test, as `grep -n` of the titles showed on 2026-11-21) pass in 20 separate `bun test` processes run beside the loaded `cli` suite; the quick repeat while iterating is `bun test --rerun-each=N test/hub.test.ts -t <pattern>`.
